What this can and cannot see

Coverage and method

The source

The ITU keeps the international list of mobile network codes under Recommendation E.212. It published the full list as it stood on 15 November 2023 as an annex to Operational Bulletin No. 1280, and every bulletin since carries numbered amendments. New Networks reads each bulletin from its PDF, applies the amendments in order, and logs the bureau's notes on the shared international ranges, which carry the effective dates the amendments omit.

What the ITU list misses

  • It holds only what national administrations notify. Some notify promptly, some in batches, some rarely.
  • Most virtual operators never get a code of their own; they run on their host network's code, so they are invisible here unless they run their own core.
  • Many private networks use shared codes. In the United States, CBRS networks share one code, 315-010, and are told apart by a sub-number the ITU never sees.
  • Dates are bulletin dates, which trail the regulator's decision by weeks or months.

Network types

Every code carries a type and a mark showing where the type came from. ● the register or a regulator states it. ◐ the holder's own name says it, such as a railway or a power company. ○ it is inferred from what is known about the holder, and about one in twenty of these may be arguable. Codes that fit none of these are left unclassified.

Cards

Changes worth explaining get a researched card. Each point on a card is marked with where it comes from: registry the bulletin or a national registry, operator the operator's own statement, reported a named news outlet, or inferred for anything unconfirmed. A card's headline never rests on an inferred point. Defence and police holders get only what the registries state.

Terms
